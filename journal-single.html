The Ultimate Weekly Chemistry Guide

Water balancing is the single most important part of maintaining your swimming pool. The chemicals you add have the crucial job of preventing organic growth, ensuring swimmer comfort, and extending the life of your filtration pump. But are you balancing them in the right order?

1. Check and Set Alkalinity

The foundation of water balance is Total Alkalinity (TA). TA acts as a buffer for your pH level. If your alkalinity is too low, your pH will fluctuate wildly. Aim to keep alkalinity between 80 and 120 PPM. Adjust this first before introducing any sanitizers.

"A balanced alkalinity buffer ensures that your chlorine can actually disinfect the pool rather than getting wasted by rapid pH fluctuations."

2. Calibrate the pH Level

Once your alkalinity is set, adjust the pH. The ideal pH range is 7.2 to 7.6. Swimmers' eyes are comfortable at this range, and chlorine is highly active. Use pH increaser (soda ash) or pH decreaser (muriatic acid) to fine-tune.

3. Sanitize with Chlorine

The final step is adding sanitizer. Chlorine keeps your pool safe by destroying bacteria and organic pathogens. Maintain free chlorine levels between 2.0 and 4.0 PPM. If your pool has a high bather load or gets heavy rain, perform a shock treatment to burn off combined chloramines.
