Weekly Guide to Shocking Your Swimming Pool

By Sarah Jenkins (CPO) Aug 12, 2026 5 min read

Shocking your pool regularly is one of the most vital tasks for maintaining sanitary, clear water. It oxidizes chloramines, bacteria, and organic waste that ordinary filters can't catch.

Many pool owners mistakenly think that running the pump and checking the chlorine level is enough. Over time, chloramines build up, creating a strong chemical odor and irritating swimmer eyes. Shocking breaks this chemical bond.

1. Choose the Right Shock Treatment

Use calcium hypochlorite (cal-hypo) or sodium dichlor for deep sanitation, or non-chlorine shock (potassium monopersulfate) for weekly maintenance when you want to swim immediately after treatment.

2. Add Shock at Dusk

Unshielded UV rays from the sun degrade free chlorine rapidly. Always dissolve and add your pool shock after sunset to maximize its sanitizing power throughout the night.

CPO Technician Pro Tip

"Always check your pool pH before shocking. Pool shock is most effective when pH is between 7.2 and 7.4. If your pH is too high, the chlorine's sanitizing power is reduced by up to 50%, wasting your chemical supply."
